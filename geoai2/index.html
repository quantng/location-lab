Network AI: traffic sensors pass messages

207 highway speed sensors in Los Angeles (the METR-LA dataset used to test graph neural networks). Lines join sensors that sit close along the road. Click a sensor, then add rounds: in each round a graph neural network lets every sensor take in information from the sensors one step away.

Read place names in text

Geo-language AI finds the places mentioned in text and puts them on a map (geoparsing). Edit the text or pick a sample, then press Find places. Names in the lab's gazetteer are matched at once; other capitalised phrases can be looked up in OpenStreetMap.

Sharper images: what generative AI must create

Free satellite images (Sentinel-2) see 10 m per pixel. Aerial images see under 1 m. Super-resolution models generate the missing detail from the coarse image. Slide between the two to see how much must be created.

At 10 m, one pixel covers a house plot; at under 1 m, you can count windows and cars. A super-resolution model must invent that detail, so its output is checked against real high-resolution images.

AI weather: three forecasts for any point

Click anywhere on the map. The app fetches the next 7 days from ECMWF's AI model (AIFS) and two physics models (ECMWF IFS and NOAA GFS), live from Open-Meteo. Where the lines agree, the forecast is more certain.
