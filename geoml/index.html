One city, one question

Why does a home near Beverly Hills cost about three times one in Compton? Each shape is a census tract (2,006 in the Los Angeles basin). Every step in this tab answers the same question with a stronger tool.

Home value: median value of owner-occupied homes (the Census caps it at $2m). Income: median household income. Density: people per km². Distances in km.

Global Moran's I: are neighbours alike?

Click a tract. Its 8 nearest neighbouring tracts are outlined, and the dot for that tract lights up in the scatter: its own value against its neighbours' average. Moran's I is the slope of the line.

Ordinary regression and its errors

log(home value) is regressed on income, distance to the coast, distance to Downtown LA and density. The map shows where the model is wrong. Red: priced above the model. Blue: priced below.

Spatial lag and spatial error models

Both models add the neighbours. The lag model lets a tract's price depend on its neighbours' prices; the error model lets unmeasured local factors be shared. Switch the map to see the errors before and after.

Local Moran (LISA): where are the clusters?

One Moran's I per tract. A tract is in a cluster when it and its neighbours are significantly alike (p < 0.05, 999 permutations). Click a class to show it alone.

Getis-Ord Gi*: hot spots and cold spots

For each tract, add up home values of every tract within 3 km, itself included, and compare with what chance would give. Significantly high sums are hot spots; low sums are cold spots.

Geographically weighted regression

GWR fits one regression per tract, using its 70 nearest tracts with weights that fall with distance. Each variable gets its own map of local effects (standardised coefficients).

Random forest: predict tracts it never saw

400 decision trees each learn rules from the data and vote. Every prediction here is for a tract held out of training (5-fold cross-validation). Add more geography to the inputs and watch the errors shrink.

Clustering: neighbourhood types without labels

k-means groups tracts by income, home value, density and distance to the coast (all standardised). Nobody tells it where anything is. Change k and run it again in your browser.

Classification: which tracts are prime?

Prime means the top quarter of home values (above $1.03m). A random forest classifier labels each held-out tract prime or not.

Satellite embeddings: what the satellite sees

An AI foundation model (AlphaEarth) turns a year of satellite data into 64 numbers per 10 m pixel. Averaged per tract, they group the city into six looks without any price data. Click a tract to find the tracts that look most like it.
