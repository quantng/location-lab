Spatial data in seven moves

Recap of the last session on one dataset: load, measure, filter, join, bin, smooth and read a satellite image.

Words you need for this recipe
Spatial data
Any record that also says where on Earth it is.
Haversine distance
The shortest distance between two latitude–longitude points over the curved Earth.
Spatial join
Linking rows because their shapes relate (a point inside an outline), not because they share an ID.
H3
Uber’s global grid of numbered hexagons; finer resolutions split each cell into about seven.
NDVI
(near-infrared − red) ÷ (near-infrared + red), per satellite pixel, from −1 to +1. Higher means greener.

Neighbours and spatial lag

Before testing any pattern, decide who counts as a neighbour. Everything after this reuses that choice.

Words you need for this recipe
Spatial weights
A table that says, for every feature, who its neighbours are. Choices: the k nearest, everyone within a distance, or features that touch.
Row-standardised
Each feature’s neighbours share a total weight of 1, so “neighbours’ average” is a fair average whatever the number of neighbours.
Spatial lag
The neighbours’ average of a value: “how is my neighbourhood doing?”

Is the pattern random?

Compare the real cafés with what pure chance would give, at many distances.

Words you need for this recipe
Complete spatial randomness
The benchmark: points scattered by pure chance over the same area.
Nearest-neighbour ratio R
Observed average distance to the nearest point ÷ the distance expected if random. Below 1 clustered, above 1 dispersed.
Ripley’s L
Repeats the comparison at many distances, so you see at which scale clustering happens. The grey band is what 19 random patterns produce.

Autocorrelation and hotspots

Do busy hexagons sit next to busy hexagons? Where exactly are the significant hotspots?

Words you need for this recipe
Spatial autocorrelation
Whether similar values sit next to each other (Tobler: near things are more related than distant things).
Moran’s I
One number for the whole map. Positive: similar values cluster. Near zero: random. Negative: neighbours differ.
Permutation test
Shuffle the values across locations many times; if the real I beats almost all shuffles, the pattern is not chance.
LISA
Local Moran’s I for each feature: High-High hotspots, Low-Low coldspots, High-Low and Low-High outliers.
Getis-Ord Gi*
Is the total around a feature (itself included) much higher or lower than chance? Reported as a z-score: 1.96 is 95% confidence.

From samples to surfaces

Estimate a value everywhere from a few samples, then check against the truth.

Words you need for this recipe
Interpolation
Estimating a value everywhere from values measured at a few places.
IDW
Inverse distance weighting: nearer samples count more (weight = 1 ÷ distance^power).
Variogram
How quickly similarity fades with distance. The range is where neighbours stop being informative.
Kriging
Uses the variogram to predict each cell and also gives a standard error map: where the estimate is reliable and where it is a guess.

What explains café density?

Regress café counts on access, then check whether space is still hiding in the errors.

Words you need for this recipe
OLS
Ordinary least squares regression: one coefficient per variable for the whole map. Assumes errors are independent.
Residual Moran’s I
If the errors themselves cluster, OLS is missing something spatial.
Spatial lag model
Adds the neighbours’ y as a predictor (ρ): spillover between neighbours.
Spatial error model
Lets errors be correlated between neighbours (λ): shared factors the model does not measure.
GWR
Geographically weighted regression: a separate regression around every location, so each effect can vary across the map.
AIC
A score for comparing models on the same data; lower is better.

Autocorrelation and hotspots

Where are prices significantly high or low, given their neighbours?

Words you need for this recipe
Spatial autocorrelation
Whether similar values sit next to each other (Tobler: near things are more related than distant things).
Moran’s I
One number for the whole map. Positive: similar values cluster. Near zero: random. Negative: neighbours differ.
Permutation test
Shuffle the values across locations many times; if the real I beats almost all shuffles, the pattern is not chance.
LISA
Local Moran’s I for each feature: High-High hotspots, Low-Low coldspots, High-Low and Low-High outliers.
Getis-Ord Gi*
Is the total around a feature (itself included) much higher or lower than chance? Reported as a z-score: 1.96 is 95% confidence.

Singapore · Recipe 7

What explains resale prices?

Does price per m² rise near MRT, hawker centres and the CBD, and is that effect the same island-wide?

Words you need for this recipe
OLS
Ordinary least squares regression: one coefficient per variable for the whole map. Assumes errors are independent.
Residual Moran’s I
If the errors themselves cluster, OLS is missing something spatial.
Spatial lag model
Adds the neighbours’ y as a predictor (ρ): spillover between neighbours.
Spatial error model
Lets errors be correlated between neighbours (λ): shared factors the model does not measure.
GWR
Geographically weighted regression: a separate regression around every location, so each effect can vary across the map.
AIC
A score for comparing models on the same data; lower is better.
